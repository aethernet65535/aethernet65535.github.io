My March
========

March 2026 has been a milestone for me. I've learned aton, but I've also
made many stupid mistakes.

"Some things are up to us, and some things are not up to us. Accept what
you cannot change, and MASTER what you can."

TL;DR
-----

- Became a "soon-to-be" DAMON contributor.
- Got the hang of the LKML house rules.

Details
-------

In 3/8, while lurking around the LKML [1], I stumbled upon a term ---
DAMON. I asked an LLM what DAMON was and discovered it's a relatively
new subsystem, merged around 2021. If you're curious about the details,
I'd recommend playing around with damo [2] alongside the source code and
docs. In my opinion, it's perfectly fine for the new-school to leverage
LLMs, provided they retain the ability to think for themselves.

Okay, continue. After I discovered DAMON is a useful feature, I went to
read the docs directly, and I found a TYPO in the doc. It made me very
excited, because it may make me able to become an Official Linux
Contributor. Then, I go to learn how to use 'git send-email' to send the
patch. It was hard to me for the first time, but I send it successfully
in the end [3].

After I sent it, I suddenly realized that the Maintainer had actually
fixed it a few hours earlier. I quickly sent a new email (a reply) to
apologize. And afterwards, I even realized that I had misspelled my name
(I wrote my real name in the email, but sent it using my online name).

However, the maintainer named SeongJae Park didn't blame me for my
carelessness. On the contrary, he even told me that patch conflicts are
very normal and there was no need to apologize. After seeing SJ's reply,
I thought he was a particulary kind and gentle person, so I decided that
I must study DAMON diligently to contribute to the subsystem he
maintains, whether it's documentation or code.

During this month, SJ taught me many house rules about the LKML. I also
fixed quite a few things this month, including two admin-guide docs [4]
[5] and a performance improvement by ilog2() [6]. I am currently working
on a task to convert aysnchoronous commits to synchronous commits,
mainly to make error feedback more timely. However, I have already done
7 versions, and I am very dissatisfied with myself. I think this is an
unprofessional performance.

Furthermore, recently, a person named Josh Law has emerged in the LKML.
He appears to be just an Openclaw Bot. He has released ~300 patches in
11 days, spanning various subsystems. Many Maintainers believe he is
just a bot and are even considering banning him.

And just now, I submmitted a patch [7] that didn't fix anything. I
initially thought it fixed a problem, but after testing, I realized I
was wrong. However, before submitting it, I didn't consider the
possibility of any issues after removing it during testing. Now, SJ hasn;
t replied to me for several hours, although it's possible he's in a flow
state and fixing a deadlock issue. But I'm still worried that he might
think I'm an irresponsible contributor, or worse, that he might think
I'm a AI Bot like Josh Law.

However, these anxieties won't change anything. The only thing I can do
is wait. And in the process of waiting, I will continuously improve
myself. That way, I can continue to contribute to DAMON. I will just
assume that SJ is simply busy with something.

SJ just replied to me. He didn't blame me for my impulsiveness, but
it;s a bit of a shame he didn't seem to see my Self-Reply. So I sent him
a thank-you reply, explaining what my Self-Reply said and including two
links to those Self-Replies. The main goal is to reduce unexpected
termination of kdamond due to user input.

While it might be a bit intrusive on his privacy, I seem to have noticed
an interesting pattern. SJ seems to only reply at 10PM and 8AM UTC+8. Of
course, it's also possible tha DAMON doesn't have many active
contributors, so he;s more laid-back. However, after discovering this
pattern, I no longer need to wait for a reply; I just check my email at
those two times.

Overall, it's been a very fruitful month. I hope that next month, my
patch will move from the AKPM tree to the Linus tree, officially merging
into the mainline, making me a Official Linux Contributor. If I become a
Linux Contributor, I will but a HHKB keyboard when it's on sale; I think
it's a keyboard for Hackers. (Non-hackers also can buy it!!)
